# Intake question bank

Ask in rounds of 2–4. Skip anything already known. Offer choices where natural; the student can always type their own answer.

## Round 1 — The degree
- Which degree? (MSc · MPhil · PhD · Other)
- University, faculty and department?
- How long must the research plan cover? (one semester · one year · two years · 3+ years)
- When is the proposal or topic due, and to whom?

## Round 2 — The student
- What do you do day to day (job, role)?
- What did you study before, and what are you strongest at?
- Which tools, languages or methods do you already use?
- What equipment, labs, devices, datasets or workplace access can you use?
- Which skills feel hard right now? (formal proofs · statistics · academic writing · programming · reading many papers · none)

## Round 3 — Wishes and limits
- Which fields excite you? Any topic you definitely do NOT want?
- Hours per week for research? (under 5 · 5–10 · 10–20 · 20+)
- Budget: free tools only, or some money for hardware/compute?
- Can you work with human participants or personal data, or should we avoid ethics approval?
- Long-term goal? (PhD · publications · industry job · start a company · finish well and move on)
- Do you want a "new algorithm/method", a system, an empirical study, or are you open?

## Round 4 — Supervisors
- Who would you like to work with, or who is available?
- For each: full name, title, university, and any link (staff page, Google Scholar, ORCID, LinkedIn).
- Has anyone already agreed? Are there department rules on who can supervise?
- If unknown: share the department staff page and I'll find the best fit.

## Round 5 — How you'll be judged
- Attach or paste the course outline, seminar slides, proposal template or marking scheme.
- Must the thesis produce a publication? Is there a viva/defence?
- Any length or structure rules for the proposal?

## Closing check
Summarise the student in 6–10 lines (degree, time, strengths, weaknesses, resources, wishes, limits, supervisors, criteria) and ask: "Is this right? Anything to add?"
