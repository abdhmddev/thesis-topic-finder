# Templates

## Default scorecard (when the university gives none)
Score each candidate 1–5:
| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Problem evidence | Is there evidence a reviewer can inspect (failure, data, standard, incident)? |
| Relevance | Scientific, technical and contextual (local/industry) relevance? |
| Originality | A reasoned difference from prior work, cleared by the fact-check? |
| Supervisor fit | Does it extend a supervisor's line? Can someone local check the method? |
| Feasibility | Data/access, method, ethics, time and money for THIS student? |

Four levels of a topic: **area → problem → question → topic**.

## Problem statement (six parts)
> In **<context>**, **<approach>** is used/recommended. However, **<limitation of existing work>**. As a result, **<consequence>**. This study will **<method>**, with the expected outcome of **<measurable outcome>**.

## Research questions and hypotheses
- RQ1 (Research Question 1): can <design> achieve <property> under <conditions>?
- RQ2: compared with <baselines>, does <design> improve <metric>?
- H0 (null hypothesis): no difference in <metric> between <design> and <baselines>.
- H1 (alternative hypothesis): <design> improves <metric> over <baselines>.

## Proposal outline (≈4 pages)
1. Title · student · supervisor · course
2. Research area
3. How the idea was found
4. Problem statement
5. Review of closest work (the matrix)
6. Research gap
7. Research questions
8. Hypotheses
9. Objectives
10. Methodology (design, tools, data, baselines, metrics, ethics)
11. Expected contribution
12. Timeline
13. Key references
14. Summary in the student's language

## Supervisor pitch deck (≈7 slides)
1. **Cover**: title, one-line subtitle, student, proposed supervisor
2. **The story**: the problem as a 20-second story anyone can follow
3. **Why now**: standards, deadlines, new technology
4. **The danger / cost**: what goes wrong today, with evidence
5. **The gap**: the matrix in one picture; what nobody has done
6. **The method**: design → check → build → test → compare
7. **The ask**: "Would you supervise this? What should I narrow or verify first?"
Each slide gets speaker notes.

## One-page concept note
Title · student · supervisor · Area · Problem and evidence (with 2–3 bullets) · Research question · Objectives · Intended contribution · Data/tools/resources · Timeline and open uncertainties · Key sources.
